# Discrete/diffusion language-model literature map

This map synthesizes the supplied three-level arXiv mine. Claims below are tied to the cited papers; numerical results are not treated as interchangeable when models, token budgets, samplers, hardware, or metrics differ.

## Training objective

The lineage begins with categorical corruption and denoising. D3PM formalizes transition-matrix diffusion over discrete states, including absorbing `[MASK]`, uniform, and structured kernels, and combines a variational bound with an auxiliary denoising loss ([Austin et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03006)). Continuous-time extensions replace discrete steps with CTMCs and train a continuous-time ELBO, with tau-leaping and predictor-corrector samplers ([Campbell et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14987); [Sun et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16750)).

SEDD instead estimates local ratios of the data distribution with a score-entropy objective, avoiding a vocabulary-sized dense transition matrix ([Lou et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16834)). MDLM makes absorbing-mask diffusion especially implementable: its SUBS parameterization yields a Rao–Blackwellized continuous-time objective that is a weighted mixture of masked-LM cross-entropies ([Sahoo et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07524)). GIDD generalizes the marginal noising distribution so masked and uniform diffusion lie in one family; in the reported small-model studies, mask-only models generally have better likelihood/downstream results, while uniform noise is a plausible **hypothesis** for improved token-constrained scaling ([Gulrajani et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04482v2); [Scaling Behavior of DLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10858)).

Adjacent objectives clarify design space rather than establish diffusion results. Diffusion-LM and SSD-LM diffuse continuous embeddings/simplex representations ([Li et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14217); [Han et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.17432)); GLM, CM3, InCoder, and FIM train causal or autoregressive span infilling ([Du et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10360); [Aghajanyan et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.07520); [Fried et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05999); [Bavarian et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.14255)). These are useful controls for context packing, span selection, and retaining left-to-right ability, but should not be mislabeled as discrete diffusion.

Post-training is becoming diffusion-native. SEPO applies policy gradients to discrete diffusion with self-normalized importance sampling, clipped ratios, and optional KL/corrector terms ([Fine-Tuning Discrete Diffusion Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01384)). d1 adapts GRPO to masked dLLMs through one-step unmasking and verifiable rewards, while VRPO reduces ELBO-based DPO estimator variance through Monte Carlo allocation and antithetic sampling ([d1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12216); [LLaDA 1.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19223)). DiffuCoder’s coupled-GRPO uses complementary mask noise for paired completions. These reported gains are promising, but increased sampling/estimation compute and limited data, task, and model coverage make broad conclusions a **hypothesis**.

## Scaling

Autoregressive baselines establish why token/model allocation must be measured rather than assumed. Kaplan et al. find power-law loss trends and model-heavy compute allocation ([Scaling Laws](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361)); Chinchilla instead finds near-equal optimal scaling of parameters and training tokens and validates a smaller, more data-rich model ([Training Compute-Optimal LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556)). Gopher and PaLM demonstrate the systems cost of frontier dense training ([Gopher](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.11446); [PaLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02311)).

For diffusion LMs, MDM reports a loss scaling rate similar to AR models but an approximately 16× compute gap at matched validation loss; its 1.1B model is competitive on selected zero-shot tasks and can sample faster under a selected conditional setup ([Scaling up MDMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18514)). Plaid reports a larger, roughly 64× matched-likelihood disadvantage for an earlier likelihood-based diffusion approach ([Likelihood-Based Diffusion LMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18619)). These gaps are not contradictory: objectives, model families, likelihood estimators, and samplers differ.

More recent studies move beyond the small-model regime but remain heterogeneous. DiffuLLaMA adapts AR checkpoints to diffusion and reaches 7B; LLaDA reaches 8B from scratch; DiffuCoder trains a 7B code model; Mercury reports trillion-token-scale training but omits parameter counts and uses a proprietary serving stack ([DiffuLLaMA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17891); [LLaDA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09992); [DiffuCoder](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20639); [Mercury](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17298)). Quokka fits compute- and data-constrained laws through 11B-scale runs and finds diffusion-specific tradeoffs, including 2–5× more data than AR models in its single-epoch comparison ([Training Optimal Large DLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03280)). Extrapolation beyond tested grids remains a **hypothesis**, especially for 10B+ behavior and repeated-epoch regimes.

## Inference

The central systems cost is iterative denoising: early methods use tens to thousands of network evaluations. DNDM reduces evaluations to distinct transition times without changing the trained predictor and reports large speedups on text/translation ([DNDM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09193)). Planned denoising separates a planner from a denoiser and can revisit low-confidence positions ([DDPD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06264)). Rectified flow reports few-step latent ODE generation, but its VAE representation makes transfer to token-native dLLMs a **hypothesis** ([Language Rectified Flow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16995)).

Caching and parallelism are the current practical levers. MDLM reports caching and semi-autoregressive arbitrary-length generation; Fast-dLLM introduces approximate block KV caches and confidence-aware parallel decoding; dKV-Cache delays reuse and limits refresh to stable tokens/local windows ([MDLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07524); [Fast-dLLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22618); [dKV-Cache](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15781)). Reported gains range from roughly 2× to 27.6×, but full attention is bidirectional, so cache equivalence is approximate. Batch-size plateaus, refresh intervals, sequence length, denoising steps, precision, and implementation dominate portability; H100/A100 claims cannot be projected to another GPU without measurement.

## Code generation

Code requires functional rather than lexical validation. HumanEval established executable Python pass@k and repeated-sample evaluation ([Chen et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03374)); LiveCodeBench adds release-date filtering and self-repair/execution/test-prediction scenarios ([LiveCodeBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07974)); Copilot Arena measures in-IDE pairwise preference across millions of suggestions ([Copilot Arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09328)). InCoder and FIM show that right context can improve infilling without necessarily destroying left-to-right synthesis ([InCoder](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05999); [FIM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.14255)).

The supplied dLLM evidence is strongest for masked code models: LLaDA reports HumanEval/MBPP results after SFT; DiffuCoder reports 7B training on 130B code tokens and a 4.4-point EvalPlus improvement with coupled-GRPO; LLaDA 1.5 reports gains on HumanEval and MBPP after VRPO; Fast-dLLM and dKV-Cache report speed/quality tradeoffs on HumanEval/MBPP. These should be compared with matched generation length, number of denoising steps, cache state, temperature, batch, and pass@k—not raw percentages alone.

## Hardware / ROCm implications

The source set is overwhelmingly NVIDIA-oriented: A100, A5000, 3090, H100, H800, V100, TPU, and proprietary engines. It therefore supplies planning evidence, not a validated MI210/ROCm recipe. A KUHPC pilot should first record ROCm, PyTorch, compiler/kernel, tokenizer, precision, scheduler allocation, topology, interconnect, and peak memory. **Hypothesis:** MI210 memory capacity may permit useful 7B inference or smaller training runs, but attention/workspace kernels, bf16 behavior, and communication topology could dominate before nominal FLOPs do.

Recommended staged experiment: (0) single-MI210 compatibility/memory/numerical-stability sweep; (1) reproduce one masked baseline with fixed seed, tokenizer, context, precision, optimizer, and schedule; (2) compare mask versus uniform at matched parameters, unique tokens, FLOPs, and quality protocol; (3) scale only across GPUs actually allocated by the scheduler, measuring batch, sequence length, denoising steps, and tokens/s; (4) test one cache/parallel-decoding method at a time and report quality loss, memory overhead, energy/resource use, and latency; (5) for code, combine HumanEval/MBPP with post-cutoff contamination-aware tests and sandboxed functional execution.

The minimum deliverables are a reproducibility manifest, topology/allocation report, quality-versus-denoising curve, scaling-efficiency curve, cache trade-off table, and an explicit list of comparisons invalidated by different hardware or proprietary serving software. Variational perplexity bounds must remain labeled as bounds, not exact AR perplexity.

## MoE dLLMs (2026 frontier — directly relevant to EcoReasoner PoC)

A 2026 cluster of work maps the MoE–diffusion intersection — the exact direction of our `dLLM-MoE` PoC. All three papers share one diagnosis: **token-level MoE routing + block-parallel diffusion decoding creates a memory-bound bottleneck** (many distinct experts activated per forward, few tokens accepted). They are the mandatory references for designing and evaluating the PoC.

- **[dMoE: dLLMs with Learnable Block Experts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30876)** (Feng et al., NUS, 2026). Aggregates token-level expert distributions into a *block-level* distribution that guides routing coherently, so all tokens in a block share experts without changing per-token expert count. Reduces uniquely-activated experts 69.5→16.5 (4.21×) while retaining 99.55% performance; −74–77% memory; 1.36–2.04× end-to-end latency. Base: LLaDA2.0-mini (Apache-2.0), 4× H100. Directly applicable to the token-level top-1 routing in `train_mdlm_moe.py`.
- **[LLaDA MoE v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03457)** (Zhu et al., RUC + Ant Group, 2026). First systematic scaling laws for MoE dLLMs. Findings: optimal batch size grows *faster* with compute than AR; optimal LR decays *faster*; compute allocation is near-balanced with a slight **data-side tilt** (more tokens than activated params); lower activation ratios favorable at larger scale; moderate expert granularity consistent; shared-expert fraction stable. Trains LLaDA MoE v2 **30B-A3B** from scratch on **23.5T tokens** (~65% of Qwen3 pretrain tokens) → approaches Qwen3; after SFT beats SDAR Chat on 7/8 reasoning+coding. Reinforces data-hungry regime and calibrates our PoC hyperparameters (bigger batch, faster LR decay).
- **[TEAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08404)** (PKU-SEC-Lab, 2026). Temporal–spatial consistency guided expert activation. Observations: decoded (accepted) tokens keep participating but only recent ones need experts; masked tokens have concentrated routing; acceptance order has spatial locality. Strategies: (1) delayed caching for decoded tokens, (2) hot/cold partition of masked tokens, (3) speculative exploration on hot + reroute cold to already-active experts. Up to **2.2× speedup** over vanilla MoE dLLM at negligible degradation. Assumes **AR-init + block diffusion** (SDAR/LLaDA-2.0), so less directly applicable to our from-scratch masked PoC but its temporal/spatial-consistency insight applies.

**Implications for the PoC:** (1) our token-level top-1 MoE router is the naive baseline these papers optimize — the PoC should record unique-experts-to-accepted-tokens ratio to catch the memory-bound regression; (2) block-level routing (dMoE) and temporal/spatial caching (TEAM) are the natural next increments for RQ3 (inference advantage); (3) LLaDA MoE v2's scaling laws are the hyperparameter prior (batch up, LR down) as we scale.
